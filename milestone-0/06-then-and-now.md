# 6. Then and now: the week-0 and week-3 recordings

*Milestone 0 pilot kit. Updated 23 October 2026: story names no longer point to one country, and messages go out in each learner's message language. The one place the pilot asks for a performance on purpose. It produces the gate's evidence ("the day-14 recording is better, judged blind, on at least one of accuracy, fluency or complexity, for most learners") and the clip the learner will want to play to friends. Design from the assessment lens (section 2, the anchor sample) and concept v2 (decision 4c).*

## Framing

It must feel like recording a memory, not sitting an exam (assessment lens risk). So:

- It is called "then and now" ("تسجيل قبل وبعد"), never a test.
- The learner hears why the tasks are always the same kind: "so that you can hear the difference in your own voice, and nothing else."
- Nothing is scored on screen or in any message. The learner hears both clips side by side at the end, with at most two plain-language differences, and only ones that passed the gate below.
- It can be skipped. If they skip, they are told once, honestly, that there will be no voice comparison.

## The tasks (about 3 minutes of speaking)

Two parallel forms, A and B, matched in length, structure and word frequency. **Odd IDs do form A in week 0 and form B in week 3; even IDs the reverse**, so the content never tells a rater which recording came first.

| Part | Task | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Retell | Listen to a short story (about 70 seconds, at most twice), then retell it in English without listening again | 60 to 90 seconds |
| 2. Opinion | Give your opinion on a familiar question, with reasons and one example | about 2 minutes |
| 3. Repeat | Hear 8 sentences one at a time; after each, repeat it exactly | about 1 minute |

**When:** week 0 = the day of the intake call, before the first episode. Week 3 = the day after the finale (day 15), so the finale episode is not a rehearsal of it.

### Messages to the learner (in their message language)

Week 0 (right after the intake call), Arabic:
```

٣ دقائق من الكلام. نعيده بنفس الشكل في آخر التجربة، لكي تسمع الفرق في صوتك أنت فقط. لا توجد درجة ولا أحد يحكم عليك.

١) اسمع القصة (تستطيع أن تسمعها مرتين كحد أقصى)، ثم سجّل رسالة صوتية تعيد فيها القصة بالإنجليزية بكلماتك (دقيقة إلى دقيقة ونصف). لا تسمعها مرة ثالثة ولا تكتب ملاحظات.

English:
```
One last step before your first episode 🎙️: "then and now".
3 minutes of speaking. We do the same thing again at the end of the pilot, so you can hear the difference in your own voice, and nothing else. There is no score and nobody judges you.

1) Listen to the story (at most twice), then record a voice note retelling it in English in your own words (1 to 1.5 minutes). Don't listen a third time and don't take notes.
2) Record a voice note answering this question in English, about 2 minutes, with your reasons and an example: [opinion question]

Week 3 (day 15): the same message, starting "آخر خطوة في الموسم الأول 🎙️: تسجيل "بعد". نفس المهام بالضبط، بقصة وسؤال مختلفين بنفس المستوى." / "The last step of season one 🎙️: the "now" recording. Exactly the same tasks, with a different story and question at the same level."

Send the story audio after the message, then wait for part 1 before sending the question, then the 8 sentence clips one at a time after part 2.

## Form A

**Story A** (single voice, calm, a little slower than normal; about 165 words):

> Last Monday was Daniel's first day at a new job in a big bank. He wanted to make a good impression, so he woke up early and left home at seven. But there was an accident on the road, and the traffic didn't move for forty minutes. Daniel started to worry. He called his new manager, Ms. Rivera, and explained the problem. She told him not to worry and to drive carefully. When he finally arrived, he ran into the building and took the lift to the wrong floor. A security guard helped him find the right office. Ms. Rivera smiled and introduced him to the team. At lunch, one of his new colleagues told him that she was late on her first day too, because she got lost in the car park. By the end of the day, Daniel felt much better. He decided to leave home thirty minutes earlier the next morning.

**Opinion A:** "Is it better to work for a big company or a small one? Give your reasons and one example from your life or someone you know."

**Repeat A** (one clip per sentence):
1. I like to drink coffee in the morning.
2. My brother lives near the old market.
3. She didn't call me back after the meeting.
4. If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.
5. The man who works in the shop knows my father.
6. I have never been to a football match in another country.
7. They asked me where I had parked my car before the interview.
8. If I had known about the traffic, I would have left much earlier.

## Form B

**Story B** (same voice and speed; about 165 words):

> Last Friday, Maya flew to Lisbon to visit her older sister for the weekend. She wanted to arrive before dinner, so she booked an early afternoon flight. But when she got to the airport, the screen said her flight was delayed by two hours. Maya started to worry. She called her sister, Ana, and explained the problem. Ana told her not to worry and to get something to eat. While Maya was waiting, she went to a café and left her phone on the table. A young man ran after her and gave it back. When she finally landed, Ana was waiting outside with a big smile. In the car, Ana told her that her own flight was cancelled once, and she slept at the airport. By the end of the evening, Maya felt much better. She decided to always pack a book in her bag for long waits.

**Opinion B:** "Is it better to live in a big city or a small town? Give your reasons and one example from your life or someone you know."

**Repeat B:**
1. I like to read the news at night.
2. My sister works at the new hospital.
3. He didn't answer my message last week.
4. If she calls today, I will tell her the news.
5. The woman who lives next door has two cats.
6. I have never tried food from that part of the world.
7. She asked me what time the train had left the station.
8. If we had booked earlier, we would have paid much less money.

The pairs are matched sentence by sentence: same structure, similar length, everyday words. Both stories have the same shape (a plan, a delay, a phone call, a small second problem solved by a stranger, a colleague or sister with the same story, a decision) so retells can be compared like for like.

**Making the audio once (Saturday or Sunday):** with Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS, one calm voice that is not Karim's or Mona's. With the kit's script: put the story in a text file with every line starting `Narrator:` and run `python episode_audio.py storyA.txt --voices Narrator=Charon`; for the sentences, one sentence per line and `--split` to get one file per sentence. Save to `For one M0/recordings/stimuli/`.

## Rating, blind

Two raters: Muath and, ideally, one English teacher paid for about 3 hours (15 learners x 3 parts, each pair about 3 minutes to hear). The teacher never learns names or which recording came first.

1. Name each learner's recordings `L03_w0_retell.ogg`, `L03_w0_opinion.ogg`, `L03_w0_repeat1.ogg` ... `L03_w3_retell.ogg` and so on, in one folder.
2. Run `python tools/blind_pairs.py <folder> <output folder>`. It copies each learner's week-0 and week-3 clip of the same part into a pair folder as `X` and `Y` in random order, and writes `key.csv` (keep it closed until rating is done; ideally a friend runs the script and keeps the key).
3. Each rater fills the **Ratings** tab in [pilot-tracker.xlsx](pilot-tracker.xlsx), for every retell and opinion pair: which is better (X, Y or same) on **accuracy** (fewer errors that matter), **fluency** (smoother, fewer long pauses mid-sentence, faster), **complexity** (longer sentences, more linking and subordinate clauses, wider words), and **overall**. For the repeat task, score each sentence 0 to 4: 4 exact, 3 meaning kept with small changes, 2 meaning partly kept, 1 only fragments, 0 nothing.
4. Unblind with `key.csv`; the tracker turns X/Y into week 0/week 3.

## Transcript measures

Run each recording through Scribe v2 (fillers kept) and give the transcripts to prompt R below. These back up the blind ratings; they are not shown to learners as numbers.

### Prompt R: transcript measures

```
You are scoring two speaking samples from the same learner of English (about B1; first language: [first language]): a retell of a short story and a 2-minute opinion. The transcripts are verbatim speech-to-text with fillers kept; they may contain recognition errors. You do not know which sample came first. For EACH transcript give:
1. Words spoken (excluding fillers like um, uh, er) and the recording length in seconds (given), and words per minute.
2. Fillers and restarts: count.
3. Errors: list each error with the learner's exact words and the minimal correction (no stylistic changes; skip unclear passages). Then errors per 100 words.
4. Complexity: number of clauses, number of subordinate clauses (because, when, if, that, who, which, although, so that...), and clauses per sentence-like unit.
5. For the retell only: which of these 8 story points are present: the plan; the delay; worrying; the phone call; the reassurance; the second small problem and the helper; the person with the same story; the final decision.
6. Range: list words that are outside everyday vocabulary.
Report as a table per transcript. Do not say which is better.
[TRANSCRIPT 1, length in seconds]
[TRANSCRIPT 2, length in seconds]
```

## The gate for claims (Milestone 0 version of the claim gate)

Concept v2 law 12: no claim without proof. A difference may be told to the learner only if all of these hold:

1. **Like for like:** same task type, week 0 against week 3.
2. **Both raters agree** that week 3 is better on that dimension in the blind rating, **and** the transcript measure moves the same way (for fluency: words per minute or fewer long mid-sentence pauses; for accuracy: errors per 100 words; for complexity: subordinate clauses or clauses per unit).
3. **Openable:** the learner can hear both clips.

At most two differences. If none pass: say so honestly ("two weeks is short; this is your starting line, and next time we'll compare against it"). If something got worse, say it plainly with the most likely reason the evidence supports ("you tried harder sentences this time"), and never invent a win to balance it.

**For the pilot gate**, a learner counts as **improved** if, on the retell or the opinion, both raters prefer week 3 on at least one of accuracy, fluency or complexity, and at most one of the four overall judgments (2 raters x 2 parts) prefers week 0. The tracker's Improvement tab applies this rule. The gate passes if most learners with both recordings are improved (8 of 15, or more than half of those who did both).

### Prompt F: the finale message

```
Write Salma's then-and-now message, in [the learner's message language], for this learner of English. It goes on WhatsApp with their two retell clips (week 0 and week 3).
Use ONLY the differences listed under PASSED (they passed a blind two-rater check and a transcript check). At most two. Phrase each one concretely with their own words or numbers in plain language, e.g. "In the first recording you stopped 6 times in the middle of a sentence; this time, twice." No percentages, levels, scores or grades. No praise of the person; name what they did.
If PASSED is empty, say honestly that two weeks is short and this is their starting line.
If WORSE lists something, mention it plainly with the reason given, in one sentence.
Then: one line on the "out in the world" moments from the learner file, if any; one line asking what their goal for a next season would be. Under 120 words. Never list mistakes; say what they can now do. Sign: "Salma, the show's producer (AI)", in their language.
[LEARNER NAME]
[PASSED]
[WORSE]
[OUT IN THE WORLD]
```

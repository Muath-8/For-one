# For one: the market, the gap, and where to win

*Market and competitors perspective. As of 2026-10-09. Written for Muath. Published doc (with a drawn market map): https://claude.ai/code/artifact/edae352d-9dd3-4100-9ff0-dd93c7eb65bf. Figures come from the sources at the end; anything marked "my estimate" or "my judgment" is mine.*

**The short version.** Talking to an AI tutor is turning into a free commodity: Duolingo says a video call now costs it under one cent, ChatGPT's voice is full-duplex and free, and Google Translate gives practice to a billion users. So For one cannot win on "AI conversation" or even on "AI with memory". The open space is the combination nobody sells: a teacher who makes your daily language media, remembers your language (not just your facts), and proves you improved. Sell that to adults who studied but cannot speak, priced against a human tutor rather than against an app, and build the moat on the learner model and a measured outcome, not on features.

| # | Decision | My recommendation | Alternative | Why it matters |

| 2 | What you sell | **A subscription priced against a tutor, about $19.99/month or $119/year, with a measured 12-week before/after speaking sample** | A cheaper app-priced plan (about $9.99) | Education apps keep only about $23 per payer in year one. An outcome you can show is the thing Duolingo cannot sell without exposing that streaks are not fluency. |
| 3 | Your stance on languages | **One pair until the show is loved, then add pairs through the language package** | Every language your voice provider supports (the PRD's current answer) | Every AI tutor's worst reviews are about weak voices and wrong accents outside the big languages. Breadth is the one race you cannot win against Duolingo or Google. |

Decision 1 overlaps the first rethink's "launch language" question; this lens adds a concrete market to it. If you answer only one, answer #1.

## Five things the market is telling you

**1. Talking to an AI is becoming free.** Duolingo's CEO said in August 2026 that a video call now costs "under $0.01", down from about 30 cents, and the company is moving it from the $30 Max tier into the $13 Super tier. OpenAI's GPT-Live (July 2026) is full-duplex with memory, and its mini version is free. Google Translate Practice is free, has pronunciation scoring, and about a third of Translate's billion users use Translate to learn. Implication: the call-in is necessary but it is not your moat, and it is not what people will pay you for.

**2. Duolingo owns the habit, not the outcome.** It is the best habit engine in consumer software: 84% of yesterday's active users come back today, and more than 10 million people hold a 365-day streak. But the most common complaint across 300 podcasts analysed in 2025 was that it does not make people conversational. Its bookings grew only 8% in Q2 2026, revenue per paid subscriber is about $6.80 a month, and the share price is down roughly three-quarters from its 2025 peak on fears that AI makes its product easy to replace. It is now spreading into chess, math and music. Implication: the outcome ("I can actually speak") is undefended.

**3. AI tutors are converging, and share the same complaints.** Speak, Praktika, Talkpal, Langua, Issen and a dozen others all sell roughly "talk to an AI tutor" for $15–20 a month. Their reviews repeat the same six problems: correction that is too lenient or invisible, no memory of your recurring mistakes, interrogation-style conversations, the wrong difficulty, robotic voices, and billing traps. When Praktika added real memory and planning agents, OpenAI reports its revenue doubled and day-1 retention rose 24%. Implication: memory is valued, and competitors are already adding it. Memory of facts about you is table stakes; a model of your language is not yet.

**4. People love hours of input, but nobody makes it personal.** Dreaming Spanish built a devoted community on nothing more than videos at the right level and an honest hours counter. Learners already hack NotebookLM into level-adjusted personal podcasts. I found no funded product that makes a daily episode from the learner's own interests, words and errors. Implication: your podcast is a genuine white space, and the demand is proven by people building it by hand.

**5. The money is in English for careers and in human tutors.** English is about 55% of the online language market. Two tutor lessons a week cost about $160 a month, roughly 20 times what Duolingo earns per payer. Preply, now valued at $1.2B, is building an AI memory and homework layer around its human tutors, which is half of what For one would offer natively. Implication: price against the tutor you replace, not against the app you resemble.

The warning that comes with all five: education apps keep about 2–3% of users at day 30, and a typical payer is worth about $23 in year one. Duolingo beats that with streaks and leagues, which For one has chosen not to use. Retention has to come from the show being genuinely good, which is why Milestone 0 matters more than any market argument.

## The gap

| What a learner needs | Duolingo | AI tutors (Speak, Praktika) | ChatGPT / Google | Input apps (Dreaming) | Human tutor | For one |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily input made for you | No | No | Only if you prompt it | No (same for all) | No | Yes: the show |
| A teacher that remembers your language and plans | Partial (difficulty model) | Partial (memory of facts) | No | No | Partial (if one tutor) | Yes: the learner file and planner |
| A lot of speaking | Little | Yes | Yes | No | Yes, expensive | Yes: call-in and retell |
| Proof you improved | XP and streaks, not ability | Weak scores | No | Hours watched | Tutor's opinion | Yes: your voice then and now |
| Price | $0–13 | $15–40 | $0–20 | $8–13 | $100–300 | About $20 |

The gap is not one feature. It is the job no one is hired for yet: "make me able to speak in my real life, every day, in something I actually want to listen to, and show me it is working." Each competitor owns one column. For one is the only design that puts all of them on one learner model.

## How the category winners did it, and the For one version

| Winner | Scarce thing made abundant | What changed in the economics | Distribution trick | The For one version |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Uber | A private driver | Idle cars became supply; the app owned the rider | One corner first (the Caltrain station), then the city | A private teacher plus a private studio; models are the supply, you own the learner |
| Airbnb | A room in a real home | Idle rooms became supply; trust came from reviews | Professional photos done by hand; the Craigslist cross-post | Hand-produce the first episodes (Milestone 0) and deliver into the podcast app people already use |
| Duolingo | A language course | Free, with 5–9% paying; 500+ A/B tests a quarter | Word of mouth, streaks, notifications; 80% of US users were not learning before | A new market: the people who quit apps and cannot afford tutors |
| Spotify | Music | Legal and cheap | Built to beat piracy, the free option, not the paid incumbent | Built to beat free ChatGPT plus NotebookLM, visibly, in the first session |
| Chegg (the warning) | Homework answers | AI made the answers free | SEO traffic captured by AI search | If the value is content or answers, AI takes it. Value must sit in structure, memory, outcome and relationship |

What this pattern says for For one:

- **The scarce thing.** A private teacher who knows you has always cost $100–300 a month and could never also write and produce media about your life. AI makes both abundant. That is the Uber move, and the honest pitch is "what only the rich had, for the price of an app."
- **The new market.** Duolingo grew because 80% of its US users had not been learning a language before. For one's equivalent is the large group who studied, quit an app, and never could afford or face a tutor: the stuck intermediate.
- **The real competitor is free.** Spotify won by being better than piracy. For one must be obviously better than ChatGPT voice plus NotebookLM in the very first session, which means the first episode about the learner has to be startling.
- **Treat models as supply.** Duolingo cut call cost about 30x by routing simple traffic to open-weight models. Build multi-model from day one, own the learner model and the evaluation data, and let model prices fall in your favour.
- **Start atomic.** One language pair, one country, one goal (for example English for job interviews in Riyadh). Widen only when that small network loves it.

## The positioning to take

**One line:** For one is your private language teacher and your own daily show. It knows you, makes something new for you every day, and proves you are getting better.

**Who it is for first:** adults who studied a language before, understand some of it, and cannot speak it, and who have a real reason (a career, a move, a partner's family). Not children, not total beginners, not hobbyists collecting streaks.

**How it stands against each rival:**

- **Against Duolingo:** "Duolingo makes you come back. For one makes you able to speak, and shows you." Outcome over habit.
- **Against AI tutors:** "They give you a chatbot that asks questions. For one gives you a show to listen to and a teacher with a plan." A teacher with a curriculum built from your errors, not a conversation partner.
- **Against ChatGPT and Google:** "They answer when you ask. For one shows up with something made for you, remembers every word you know, and proves progress." Structure, memory of your language, and proof.
- **Against human tutors:** "A teacher every day for the price of one lesson a month, with no fear of being judged." About 21% of learners cite embarrassment as a barrier. Later, tutors can become a bridge rather than a rival.

**Where to launch (decision 1).** My recommendation is English for Arabic speakers in the Gulf, starting with Saudi Arabia. The case: low English proficiency (Saudi Arabia scores 404 on EF's index, rank 115), a government push for English, fast-growing app spending in the Gulf (over $700M in-app in Q2 2025, up 20%), proven willingness to pay for tutors (Cambly's top four markets include Saudi Arabia), and errors that Arabic speakers share and that a tuned taxonomy can target better than a global app. The risk: English for Arabic speakers is crowded. Speak, Praktika, Loora, ELSA and local AI tutors such as Langla already rank in Saudi charts. For one would win there only on the show and the proof, not on conversation. If you cannot recruit Gulf learners yourself, pick whichever pair you can recruit 20 real learners for; that matters more than market size at this stage.

**Price (decision 2).** AI tutors cluster at $15–20 a month, Duolingo earns about $6.80 per payer, and tutors cost about $160. I would charge about $19.99 a month or $119 a year, and make the product's promise concrete: a 3-minute speaking sample on day 1 and every 4 weeks, rated blind. Lingoda's "Sprint" (money back if you attend enough classes) shows outcome-linked offers are workable. Test this in the private beta, not before.

## What incumbents cannot copy

Be honest about this first: any feature in the PRD can be copied in a quarter, and Duolingo, OpenAI and Google all have more money. Defensibility comes from things that compound over time or that a rival will not do because it would hurt its own business.

**Things that are not moats:** AI conversation (free now), memory of facts about you (ChatGPT and Praktika have it), "personalized" content as a label, and the number of languages.

**Things that can be moats, strongest first:**

1. **Counter-positioning against Duolingo's engine.** Duolingo's growth runs on streaks, leagues and notifications; about a fifth of its daily users hold a 365-day streak. For one's laws (no streaks, a 2-session daily cap, no guilt, honest flat weeks) are things Duolingo cannot adopt without damaging the metric its investors watch. Cost is no longer a barrier for Duolingo, so this has to be a business-model difference, not a price difference.
2. **A model of the learner's language, not just their life.** Every word they own and how well, every recurring error and whether it is fading, their voice over months, their history with their hosts. A general assistant stores loose facts; it does not keep a calibrated picture of someone's grammar and vocabulary. After 50 episodes, leaving For one means starting from zero with a teacher who does not know you. Replika's history shows how strong attachment to a persistent character is, and how badly users react when it is changed carelessly.
3. **Owning the outcome measure.** If For one measures speaking ability from natural conversation and proves it is accurate, that measure can become something learners show to employers or schools. Duolingo's English Test is about 10% of its business, and its CEO says he wants to "own the score for languages". Once institutions accept a score, it becomes a network effect. This is a long-term goal, not a launch feature.
4. **A teaching data loop.** Over thousands of learners, For one learns which nudge fixes which error for which native language, measured against real outcomes. Data advantages are weaker than founders expect, but this one is specific to the domain and requires the outcome measure above.
5. **Craft and trust.** Authored hosts tuned per language, and a public promise of no manipulation. In a market where Duolingo's "AI-first" memo cost it US users and AI companions are criticised for manipulative tactics, a trusted brand is a real asset.
6. **Distribution built into the product.** Episodes that arrive in the learner's own podcast app use a habit they already have. A short "my voice, week 1 versus week 8" clip is a shareable proof, the way Spotify Wrapped spreads Spotify. Paid referral credits added only 3% at Duolingo, so do not rely on them.

## Threats to watch

| Threat | How likely in 12–24 months | What it would look like | Defence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo personalises its AI podcast and gives Lily memory | High | DuoRadio episodes using your name and weak words | Depth of the learner model, honest proof, adult tone, no streak pressure |
| OpenAI or Google ship a dedicated language mode | Medium | A "learn Spanish" mode with memory and lessons | Their vertical bets have struggled; win on a structured plan, proof and craft in one language |
| An AI tutor adds personal podcasts | High | Langua or Praktika adding daily audio stories | Speed, show quality, and the call-in tied to the episode |
| Learners do not come back without streaks | Medium | Week-4 retention well under 40% | Milestone 0 and the beta gates; one learner-chosen cue |
| Dependence on one model provider | Medium | Price or policy change | Multi-model from day one; own the evaluation data |

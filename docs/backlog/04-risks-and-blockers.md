# For one: risks and decisions

*Written 2026-11-06; rewritten 2026-11-12 under ADR-25. There are no open blockers: every decision is taken in ADR-24 and ADR-25 ([architecture/10-decision-records.md](../architecture/10-decision-records.md)). What remains are Muath's own actions (A1 to A9) and the numbers the tests produce (M2 to M9), both in that document's §10, each tied to the phase that needs it. Risks are things that could delay launch or break a launch health measure, with what tests them and the fallback.*

## B. Risks

| Id | Risk | Why it matters | What tests it | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The agent's code passes its own tests but is wrong.** Tests written by the same agent from the same reading of the story can share its misunderstanding | Bugs that every check agrees with reach learners | The independent AI review pass reads the story and the laws, not the code's intent; the eval suites run on fixtures the agent did not write for that story; the phase exit checks are end-to-end | Muath reviews the acceptance tests of every learner-facing story before the code (the approval on the test commit); a failed exit check reopens the phase, not just the story |
| R2 | **Drift from the architecture.** An agent with a long backlog invents a second way to do something that already has one (a second queue, a direct vendor call, a new table) | The system stops being one system; debugging gets slow | Lint rules (no vendor SDK outside an adapter; no new table without a migration Muath approves); the AI reviewer checks each change against the architecture document for its domain | `AGENTS.md` names the one home of each concern; a review finding of "second way" is blocking |
| R3 | **Muath becomes the bottleneck.** Every merge, migration, prompt change and deploy waits for one person | Phases slow to the speed of approvals | The number of changes waiting for approval, in the morning digest | Approvals are batched per story, not per commit; low-risk changes (tests, docs, lint) merge on CI and the AI review alone if Muath sets that rule in `AGENTS.md` |
| R4 | **A prompt or bible change breaks a law unseen.** The agent edits prompts as freely as code | A host that says "wrong", a proof that overclaims, a wrong memory spoken aloud | `eval-gate` (law scan, governor replay, editor, claim gate) on every prompt change; Muath approves every prompt and bible change | The release manifest pins every prompt version; `pull` removes a bad episode; a bad prompt is rolled back as a release |
| R5 | **Security mistakes an AI reviewer misses.** Secrets, RLS gaps, injection paths, over-broad IAM | Learner data exposed | The RLS CI test, the secrets scan, the injection set, the scrub corpus, the restore and `privacy/verify` checks | Dependency and secret scans in CI, an OWASP ZAP baseline of staging, and an AI security review of every change to auth, keys, media URLs, the feed or deletion (ADR-25.11); the agent has no access to production learner data; a paid penetration test at 1,000 paying learners, before a B2B contract or after any incident; a notifiable breach is a lawyer trigger (ADR-25.3) |
| R6 | **No lawyer: a legal text or a design choice is wrong in some country.** The self-review rests on official guidance, not advice | A complaint, a regulator letter, or a fine | The self-review checklist with a source for every position; the AI review pass of every legal text; the conservative defaults (GDPR-grade rights for all, 18+, no voiceprints, voice off by default, opt-in data use, the sensitive-fact card) | The triggers in `legal/README.md`: a regulator or court contact, a notifiable breach, a B2B or employer contract, or paid traffic from a country with a specific law the checklist does not cover; each means hiring a lawyer then |
| R7 | **The EU and UK representative is missed.** GDPR Art. 27 applies if the company sits outside the EU and UK | Unlawful processing for EU and UK learners | Launch checklist line 6 | Appoint the paid service before launch (A7) or hold EU and UK signups |
| R8 | **Speech-to-text smooths the learner's errors** | The teacher never sees the mistakes it is meant to bring back | E02-S02 on the volunteer clips and the corpus; E09-S01's error-survival baseline | Choose the verbatim vendor on error survival, not accuracy; keep per-turn audio for re-transcription; never build a claim on a low-confidence turn |
| R9 | **Test data is not real learners.** Without a trial run, the vendor tests, the governor replay and the fixtures rest on 30 volunteer clips, an open corpus and synthetic learners | Thresholds and quality numbers are less certain at launch | The launch health measures at 4 and 8 weeks; the error-survival and recognizer-error rates by first language from launch | Conservative thresholds (similarity 0.85, event claims only); re-tune on real use from opted-in data; the per-language close rule (E16-S07) |
| R10 | **Learners do not fix errors when nobody says "wrong"** | Invisible teaching is the product's riskiest teaching bet (decision 3) | The 4-weekly recordings judged blind: do the errors the show worked on fall? | Raise how often the right form comes back and how often the host echoes it, still without saying it was wrong; the observer is the first thing to add after launch |
| R11 | **The host is too slow** (over 1.5 s median) | The call stops feeling like a conversation | E02-S04 and the 50-call reruns in phases 2 and 4 | Cached brief, the Haiku-host comparison (E14-S06), the runner-up voice for the call, worker placement closer to the vendor |
| R12 | **Cost per session above $0.60** | Stop line at $1.00; the 2 and 3 a day plans lose money above $0.42 | E14 tracing from the first test render; the exit document in phase 6 | Batch everything nobody waits on; the cheapest voice that passes the blind test; shorter default episode; the negotiated voice rate (A9); the ADR-24.5 price rule for new signups |
| R13 | **Wrong memories reach a host's mouth** | "A wrong memory is a bug"; target under 1 in 100 callbacks | E09-S05 fact precision on synthetic intakes; the weekly count after launch | Quote-backed facts only, the confirmation ladder, at most 2 life facts per episode, the fix path |
| R14 | **AI sameness: episodes feel alike by week 4** | The riskiest product assumption | The similarity score, the AI review of episode samples, "worth it" and week-4 activity after launch | The variety system (E04-S11), real input every day, hosts written by people, a guest host or new format in the next season |
| R15 | **iOS web: the call dies when the screen locks** | The commute use case | The device checklist in phase 6 | Calls screen-on with earphones; the dial-in experiment (P1); there is no native fallback (ADR-25.4) |
| R16 | **One person runs operations** | A 05:40 drop failure with nobody awake | The tabletop; the canary; the alert rules | Automated fallbacks first (yesterday's episode stays, the on-demand fallback at drop minus 90 minutes, the fallback episode, voice notes); phone alerts only for a systemic drop failure; runbooks the agent runs when Muath asks |
| R17 | **A second language opens too early** | Quality falls behind English and trust suffers | The gate harness (E02-S08) and the per-language close rule (E16-S07) | English only unless all five checks pass; a language that falls 15 points behind closes |

## C. Assumptions this backlog makes

1. The architecture documents are right in content; where they still carry a stale decision, ADR-24 and then ADR-25 win, and the stories follow them.
2. English is the only target language at launch; the learner's own language can be any language with interface strings, glosses and a recognizer that catches it mid-sentence; `native/en` ships, `native/ar` once Muath has read it and its blind test passed.
3. Muath is available to approve changes as the agent asks; the agent works only through the repository, CI and staging, never on production learner data.
4. The open corpus licence (speechocean762, Apache-2.0) and the volunteers' one-line consents cover the vendor tests; no real learner's data is used before launch.

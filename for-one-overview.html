A podcast made for one person, and a friend to talk to about it.

For one teaches a language without ever telling you that you got something wrong. Pick a mistake below and see how that works.

What it is

Every evening you choose what tomorrow's episode is about. Overnight the app writes and records it: a five to seven minute show, with two hosts, about something you care about, in the language you're learning. About 95% of the words are ones you already know, so you can follow it. The rest are a few new words the app has chosen for you.

When you've listened, you call the host. It's a real spoken conversation, five to seven minutes, about the episode and your life. The host never grades you or says you're wrong. If you make a mistake, the host says the right version back as part of the conversation, and the new words and forms you needed show up again in the next episodes.

The app remembers what you said, and only what you said. It uses that to plan the next day.

The episode

New every day, written just for you. Never reused. It arrives in the app and in your normal podcast app through a private link.

The call

You speak, the host answers in about a second. Speaking is where most learners freeze, so this is the main practice.

The memory

A record of everything you did and said. Each night it works out which words and grammar you need next.

It's for adults who already understand a fair amount but freeze when they have to speak. There is no points system, no streak, no level and no chart anywhere in it.

One day with the app

Click through the day. This loop repeats every day.

Once a week a short audio message, about two minutes, tells you in your own language what you can now do that you couldn't before. It only says things the app can back up. If it was a slow week, it says so. Every four weeks you record a few minutes of speaking, the same kind of task each time. Comparing it to the last one is how the app shows real progress, and it closes a four week season of the show.

Who you meet

You only ever meet three voices. Behind them is a planner you never hear.

In the target language

The hosts

Two voices who present the episode and then take your call. They're the same voices in both, so the call feels like talking to the people from the show. They are always honest about being AI.

In your own language

Salma, the producer

The face of the app. She welcomes you, runs your notebook, reads your weekly message and explains things when you ask. She never corrects you and stays silent during calls.

Always present

You

You choose the topics, the style and the characters. The app decides the teaching, and it will tell you why it picked something if you ask.

The unseen planner. Each night an AI teacher reads your memory and decides which words and grammar come back tomorrow, then briefs the writer and hosts. It never speaks to you.

What it remembers, and how

Everything starts as a log of events: what you played, which words you tapped, every sentence you said. The log is never edited. Your profile is calculated from it, and can be rebuilt from scratch at any time.

  • Words you own. Words you've used correctly on your own, on different days.
  • Grammar you're working on. Forms you get right, and forms you're still shaky on. Three slips across two sessions makes a pattern.
  • Your life. Things you said about yourself, each saved with your exact words.
  • Your interests, goals and taste.

You can open all of it in your notebook, correct any item or delete it.

Sensitive topics (health, money, relationships, religion, politics, sexuality) are only kept after you tap a confirmation card. The app never looks you up and never guesses.

The rules it keeps

The documents list twelve laws. In plain words, these are the ones that shape the product, and each one is checked automatically in the code.

No game mechanics

No streaks, points, badges, levels, percentages or confetti. Plain counts such as hours heard are allowed.

Teaching you can't see

Hosts may repeat the right form, ask for clarification, or ask you to recall something. They can't grade, explain grammar unprompted or say "wrong". A filter in the code blocks any line that does.

Nothing guilty, nothing missed

Missing a day costs nothing. Stories wait for you. Only three kinds of messages are ever sent.

A finite portion

Your plan sets how many new episodes you get per day. There is no autoplay and no endless feed. Re-listening is unlimited.

No progress claim without proof

A piece of code, not an AI, decides what the app may say about your progress. The claim has to hold up across several days of clean evidence.

Your data is yours

You can see, export and delete everything. Deleting stops access at once, live data is purged within 24 hours, backups within 30 days. Voice recordings are deleted within 15 minutes of a call unless you choose to keep them.

Languages

Two separate choices: the language you learn, and the language the app uses to talk to you.

The language you learn

English

English is the only one at launch, in British or American voices. Another language opens only when it passes five checks: natural-sounding voices, accurate speech recognition of learners' real mistakes, natural scripts, a fast call, and a grammar list.

The language of the app and Salma

Arabic or English

Menus, Salma, captions and word meanings are in this language, including right-to-left layout for Arabic. More can be added when their text and Salma's voice exist. Nothing in the code assumes a country.

How it's built

There's a website, a server, a place to do overnight work, a place to run live calls and a database. AI companies provide the thinking and the voices. Every outside service sits behind a thin adapter, so swapping one is a settings change.

Monthly only. The trial needs no card and you choose a plan on day seven. No free tier, no ads, and a full refund within 14 days of the first payment. A later price rise affects new customers only.

Each session costs roughly $0.60 in AI and voice fees at launch. The target is $0.35, and $1.00 is the stop line. Every vendor call is priced and recorded, so the real cost per session is a database query, not a guess.

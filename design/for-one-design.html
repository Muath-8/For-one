For one makes one show for one person, every day. The product is sound; the screen is the studio window. This book sets the principles, the design system, the map, the flows and the key screens so the beta can be built without reopening the product decisions.

00 · Principles

Ten rules, each with a test

Three facts shape every screen: the product is sound, teaching is invisible, and progress is evidence rather than games. The rules below follow from them, and each can be applied to a mockup and answered yes or no.

One thing, made todayThe home screen is today's episode and one button. Alternatives are one tap away and quieter.Within two seconds of opening, does the learner know the one thing to do?
Ears first, eyes optionalPlay, replay a sentence, accept the call, hold the floor, ask for a word, end: all from headphone and lock-screen controls.Can a whole session happen on a bus with the phone in a pocket?
The teacher has a voice, never a dashboardProgress arrives as Salma's sentences and the learner's own recordings.Would a good human tutor say this out loud?
Nobody is ever told they were wrongNo screen, card, colour or sound marks a learner sentence as incorrect. The right form is heard in the host's reply and returns in later episodes.Find any pixel that says "this was wrong". There must be none.
Hidden by default, open on requestThe notebook shows every fact with its source and a delete, the "working on" list of at most three, and why today's episode was made.Does every hidden decision have a place where it can be seen and corrected?
Never leave a debtNo missed days, overdue items or unread counts. A learner back after three weeks sees a day like any other, plus an easier return episode that never mentions the gap.Does any screen change because the learner was away?
A finite, good portionA session ends like a show: sting, sign-off, tomorrow's time. No autoplay, no "one more", no infinite scroll.After the session, is there anything left to scroll?
Make speaking safeHold to keep the floor; the host waits as long as needed. Hold the word button and say it in your own language; the host gives it back and carries on.What does a frozen learner do? There must be an obvious, kind answer.
Sound is interfaceA breath and a rising tone before your turn; a closer, drier voice for Salma; a sting at each end of an episode. No reward sounds, ever.With eyes closed, can the learner tell who is talking and whose turn it is?
Your life, your words, your voiceEvery example on screen is the learner's own sentence, recording, team or interview. No stock sentences anywhere.Could this screen be shown to a different learner unchanged?
Who is who on screen

Three voices and nothing else

The hosts

Karim and Mona, in the target language only. They make the episode, argue on the call, banter in the chat, echo the right form inside a reply, leave gaps for last week's words, ask for a sixty-second retell, give a word back when asked, explain only when asked.

correct, grade, mention progress, interrupt, praise the person, pretend to be human

Salma, the producer

The face of the app in the learner's own language. Onboarding, the notebook, "why this episode", the weekly proof, the season finale, the plan and the controls in plain words. Silent during calls.

correct, send notes after a call, exclaim, guilt, persuade

The learner

Picks tomorrow from three ideas, takes sides, calls in, keeps words, vetoes what the show works on. The green orb appears only on the hold pad and on the tape.

fill in forms, take tests, see a score
01 · Design system

Night studio, with a daylight version

Dark is the default because the show is heard at 7:40 on a bus and at night in bed. Colour comes from the voices, never from status: there is no green tick and no red mark anywhere near a learner's words. Tokens are in tokens.css and tokens.json.

Phone gutter 20. Reading measure 34rem. Minimum tap target 44. The hold pad is 112 tall on purpose.

Radius and motion

One easing, four durations: 120 for taps, 200 for sheets, 320 for screens, 600 for the orbs' breath and the cover reveal. Nothing about progress animates. All durations fall to zero under reduced motion.

What is not in the system, on purpose

Progress bars for learning, streak counters, badges, confetti, level labels, percentage rings, red error states on text, modals that interrupt audio, infinite lists, faces for the hosts, chip grids, explanatory text inside the app.

03 · Flows

A day, from the drop to tomorrow's choice

The episode drops at the time the learner chose. Doors open in any order; reading before the call is suggested because the article arms it. Nothing is shown after the call. Tomorrow is chosen tonight.

Where the time goes

Estimated share of a learner's time by mode, from the UI/UX lens. It is why lock screen and headphones come before any screen.

Ears~65% Voice~20% Eyes~15%

The first ten minutes (order A)

Sound in under a minute, the first spoken sentence within five, the account only once there is something to keep. Order B (account first) is designed too; the steps are the same and the order is configurable for the pilot.

02 · Information architecture

Three places, four modes, one feed

Today, Show and Notebook are places. The player, the call, the proof and the finale are modes that hide the navigation. Everything not on this map is a sentence to Salma.

Web app plus feed

Same screens. Adds a "keep the screen on" hint before calls and an install card once. The feed carries lock-screen listening. Web checkout keeps about 96% of revenue.

Native app

Same screens. Adds lock-screen call accept, hands-free calls, guaranteed headphone actions and CarPlay Now Playing. Store checkout keeps about 85%. Open decision; both are designed.

05 · Interaction and audio

Making speaking safe, in milliseconds

The call is where learners freeze and where consumer assistants fail them by jumping in. The host waits longer than any assistant, never barges in, and the silence after the learner's turn is covered by breath and fillers in the host's own voice, never by a spinner.

Hold the pad

While held, the host does not speak, however long. Release hands the turn back after 400ms so a late syllable is not cut.

The word button

Hold, say the word in your own language, release. The host gives it back inside its next line and carries on. Salma is silent in calls.

The echo

"Oh, you went there yesterday?" is the only correction the learner ever meets. Heavier weight in the caption, no label, no tap target.

07 · Open

Designed both ways, waiting on a decision

01
Web app plus feed, or native, for the betaBoth designed on the same screens. The pilot's commute data settles it: calls abandoned on the move and the feed-to-call handoff.
02
First-session orderSound in 60 seconds with the account after the first call, or account first. Built as a configurable step order so the pilot can compare drop-off.
03
Who writes the host and Salma biblesThe content guidelines are the brief; the model writes episodes inside the bibles and never writes the bibles. An owner is needed before Nov 18.
04
Spoken talkbackButton-only in the beta. Voice ("how do you say…") waits for the per-language speech stack to catch code-switching reliably.
05
Dark only, or dark and lightBoth exist in the tokens at little cost; dark is the default. Learner preference in the beta decides whether light stays.

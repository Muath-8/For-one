Security, privacy, consent and compliance

How the promises about voice and life facts become verifiable: logged consent, deletion jobs with independent checks, staff access the learner can see, and a crisis path.

Unreviewed first draft 15 decisions 11 open for Muath 16,240 words, about 70 min

Unreviewed first draft. Nobody has checked this draft against the PRD yet. Treat every number as provisional.

In one paragraph

The product holds the two things a person is least willing to lose control of: their voice and the facts of their life. The architecture already isolates every learner by row (Postgres RLS) and by key (one data key per learner wrapping every audio object), so this document's job is to make the promises in PRD 1.3 and 12.5 verifiable rather than merely implemented: every consent is a versioned, logged event; every deletion window is a job that writes its own evidence and a second, independent job that checks it; every staff look at learner content is a logged, reasoned act the learner can see in their export; every live character has a fixed, counsel-reviewed path out of the show when a learner is in danger; and every vendor that touches voice or text is named, with what it sees, where, and for how long. For a one-to-two-engineer team the rule is: managed primitives (KMS, RDS encryption, S3 SSE-C, pgaudit, the processor's hosted checkout) and small tables, no security products to operate.

The decisions

Section 1's table, one card per decision. "Rejected" is what was considered and turned down.

  1. D1

    Key model

    Accept 01's envelope: one KMS CMK per environment wraps a 32-byte DEK per learner (learner_keys); S3 SSE-C for every learner-scoped audio object; RDS and backups encrypted under KMS; no column-level encryption in the beta [DECISION, PRD 12.6]

    Why Key deletion deletes audio at $1/month; Range reads work; two engineers cannot run app-side crypto safely

    Rejected KMS key per learner (cost at 10k); pgcrypto columns for life facts (breaks rebuilds and search)

  2. D2

    Who may unwrap a DEK

    Only the three task roles, through forone.core.keys.get_dek(learner_id, purpose, actor), which writes an audit_log row per unwrap; a human reaches a DEK only through a break-glass IAM role with MFA, logged in CloudTrail [DECISION]

    Why The unwrap is the one chokepoint for voice; an audit row per unwrap (not per read) is cheap and complete

    Rejected Unrestricted KMS Decrypt for engineers; no purpose tagging

  3. D3

    Produced audio and a CDN

    In the beta, every learner-scoped object stays SSE-C, including show audio; after the beta, kinds that contain no learner voice (episode_segment, episode_full, read_aloud, recap, feed_tag) may move under an SSE-KMS service key behind signed CDN URLs if the 2 s start budget needs it; learner-voice kinds never [DECISION, answers 01 §9.6]

    Why 01 and 06 meet the 2 s budget with prefetch; one key model in the beta is one fewer thing to get wrong

    Rejected A CDN now (no measured need)

  4. D4

    Consent as data

    Six consent kinds, each a versioned copy in the language package (consent_texts.yaml), each change a consent_change event carrying text_shown_version, mirrored into consents (04) and relayed as learner.consent_changed; no consent is inferred from behaviour [DECISION, PRD 2.2 A9, 12.4, 12.5]

    Why PDPL asks for consent that is specific, informed and provable; a version id per shown text proves it

    Rejected A single "I agree" at signup; consent flags without copy versions

  5. D5

    Deletion windows as verifiable jobs

    The doers (audio/expire, learner/purge-audio, learner/delete, retention/daily) each write a privacy_audit_runs row with counts and a verification method; an independent daily privacy/verify function re-checks every window from the data alone and raises privacy.audit_failed; /admin/privacy shows the last 30 days [DECISION, PRD 5.4.11, 12.5, 15]

    Why "Within 15 minutes" is a promise a learner cannot check, so the system must check it with code that does not share the doer's bugs

    Rejected Trusting the job's own success status; manual monthly audits

  6. D6

    Research set anonymization

    Text only; pseudonym = HMAC-SHA256(RESEARCH_PSEUDONYM_SECRET, learner_id); display name, life_facts.people names, emails, phone numbers, URLs and digit runs of 5+ replaced by typed tokens; place names kept; rows removed on revoke or deletion; read only through /admin/research by the tagger role, never bulk-exported [DECISION, PRD 12.5.5, 04 D18]

    Why Enough to stop a tagger recognizing a person, cheap enough to run inside the after-pass

    Rejected Full de-identification by a model (cost, and it rewrites the learner's grammar, which is the data)

  7. D7

    Home region and transfers

    Accept me-central-1 (UAE) as the default; the AWS Saudi region replaces it if generally available by 2026-11-16 [ASSUMPTION]; model, voice and recognition vendors process in the US and EU regardless, so the consent copy and the processor register (4.9) carry the cross-border disclosure; counsel confirms the transfer basis [DECISION, PRD 12.6, 18 #3]

    Why Residency of data at rest is one config key; the transfers that matter for PDPL are the vendor ones, and those are the same in every region option

    Rejected Moving everything to GCP Dammam now (two to three days, no confirmed requirement)

  8. D8

    Age gate

    A1 checkbox stored as learners.age_attested_at; birth date validated server-side at A9 (403 under_18), and a refused pre_account learner is deleted at once; abandoned pre_account learners deleted after 24 h; no identity verification in the beta [DECISION, PRD 1.4, 2.2 A1, A9]

    Why Matches the PRD; the pre-account voice (name clip, intake) must not linger without a consent

    Rejected Nafath or ID verification (later, if counsel requires)

  9. D9

    Crisis protocol

    One crisis_log table and one Inngest event crisis.activated fed by five triggers (host @moves.crisis, package keyword list, Salma talkback, chat host flag, analyst flag); fixed counsel-reviewed clips and a resources card from the package; the triggering turn is quarantined from facts, notes, canon and proof; counts-only annual report query [DECISION, PRD 7.9]

    Why Two independent live triggers, fixed text, and a quarantine so the show never "remembers" the worst moment

    Rejected Generated crisis replies; model-only detection; a content-bearing report

  10. D10

    Prompt-injection posture

    Three trust tiers for prompt text; every learner-authored string wrapped in <listener_said> with a fixed operator rule and sanitized (markers, control characters, length); hosts and Salma have no tools; an output guard in code before any synthesis or send (AI-disclosure guard, law-word scan, three-refusals rule); invariant: no learner-authored text enters a shared store [DECISION, PRD 1.2 law 5, 6.6, 12.6]

    Why The hosts hold nothing worth stealing except the learner's own brief; the real risks are the show breaking its own laws on request and cross-learner leakage, both of which code can check

    Rejected Classifier-in-the-loop on every turn (latency); trusting the model's own refusals alone

  11. D11

    Abuse and cost-abuse controls

    Rate limits in a Postgres rate_limits table (no Redis) per the table in 4.11; a per-learner, per-local-day cost_guard (soft $2.50, hard $5.00) that pauses on-demand generation but never playback; one free trial per identity hash per 180 days [DECISION, PRD 1.4, 3.6, 14.5]

    Why The allowance already bounds a learner to one episode, one swap, one call, one round two per day; the guard catches the loops and the fallbacks that the allowance does not

    Rejected Redis-backed limiter (one more service); hard-stopping the whole account

  12. D12

    Payments

    Hosted checkout only (SAQ-A scope); signed webhooks with replay protection into payment_events and a subscriptions state machine; VAT invoices from the processor; founding-price counter enforced in api; processor OPEN, Stripe test mode as the week-3 scaffold [DECISION, PRD 1.4, 18 #2]

    Why No card data ever reaches us; the adapter in 01 §4.5 is enough

    Rejected Custom card forms; storing processor PDFs

  13. D13

    Audit logging

    One append-only audit_log for staff, service and system actions on learner data (not learner behaviour, which is events); pgaudit on the forone_readonly role; staff-access rows included in the learner's export [DECISION, PRD 1.3 #5, 6.8]

    Why The learner can answer "who looked at my data" from their own export

    Rejected Logging to CloudWatch only (not learner-visible)

  14. D14

    Staff access

    Named staff_accounts with email OTP and a role (owner, engineer, reviewer, tagger, ops), IP allowlist kept from 01, 12 h sessions, per-role pages; the reviewer sees scripts with the display name tokenized and never sees transcripts or the notebook; break-glass reads of audio require a reason [DECISION, PRD 6.8]

    Why Basic auth cannot say who; a reviewer is a paid outsider

    Rejected Shared basic auth (01); a third-party admin product

  15. D15

    Incident response

    Four severities, one runbook, a security_incidents table, the 72-hour regulator window as the clock, a tabletop in build week 5 [DECISION, PRD 12.7]

    Why A small team needs one page it has rehearsed once

    Rejected An unrehearsed policy document

  4. Processors to approve (by 2026-11-16)

    Langfuse Cloud EU with names tokenized and 30-day retention; Resend with tracking off; Sentry EU; Vercel; LiveKit Cloud; Inngest EU with ids only; the verbatim recognizer with minimum retention (the vendor that sees the most voice). Say no to any and section 4.9 names the replacement; Langfuse is the only expensive one (about three days and $60 a month to self-host). Also: request Anthropic zero-data-retention for the API organization.

  5. Payment processor and entity (2026-11-16, PRD 18 #2)

    Mada needs a Saudi-capable processor (Tap, Moyasar, HyperPay); Stripe needs an entity in a Stripe country. The design is neutral; the swap costs about three engineer-days if it is not Stripe (01).

  6. Trial without a card (PRD 18 #4)

    this document assumes no card and limits the free week to one per identity per 180 days; if you prefer card-first, the trial ledger is unnecessary and checkout moves to A9.

  7. Research rows after deletion

    this document deletes them (04 D18). Confirm.

  8. Second factor for staff (passkeys) and a penetration test

    both P1; the test costs about $5,000 to $10,000 and belongs before public launch in month 4, not before the beta. Approve or defer.

  9. Milestone 0 import consent (01 §10 #11)

    the week-5 end-to-end run uses Milestone 0 learners' data on the production system. Either the Milestone 0 consent covers it (counsel) or a one-line WhatsApp re-consent is sent in week 4, as 02-consent.md's "two later asks" pattern already does for the founding offer.

  10. Waitlist retention

    the waitlist holds names, WhatsApp numbers or emails, city and a reason; this document proposes deleting rows not invited within 12 months of collection and says so on the form. Confirm the period.

  11. Age verification beyond self-attestation

    none in the beta; if counsel requires it for a paid subscription, Nafath at checkout is the candidate (P1 at the earliest).

4.1 Encryption and key management (PRD 12.6; implements 01 D7)

At rest. RDS storage, automated backups and snapshots: encrypted with the environment CMK forone-{env}-data. S3: every object in forone-media and forone-voice that carries a learner_id is SSE-C under that learner's DEK; forone-exports is SSE-KMS under the same CMK (the zip inside is passphrase-encrypted, 04 D16); package assets (bibles, clips, lists) are SSE-S3 in forone-media/package/. Bucket policies deny any PutObject without x-amz-server-side-encryption-customer-algorithm or x-amz-server-side-encryption set, deny public access, and deny all principals except the three task roles, the CI deploy role (package prefix only) and the break-glass role.

In transit. TLS 1.2 or higher everywhere; HSTS max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains; preload on forone.app; the ALB terminates TLS with an ACM certificate; call-worker to api inside the VPC over HTTPS with the service token (01); WebRTC media is DTLS-SRTP by LiveKit; no plaintext listener anywhere.

import base64, hashlib, os, time
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Literal

Purpose = Literal["call", "verbatim", "playback", "export", "proof_clip", "notes_clip", "relisten",
                  "pronunciation", "admin_break_glass", "migration"]

@dataclass
class Dek:
    key: bytes            # 32 bytes
    md5_b64: str          # S3 SSE-C needs base64(md5(key)) on every request
    key_b64: str
    version: int

_cache: dict[str, tuple[Dek, float]] = {}      # learner_id -> (dek, expires_at); process-local, 10 min
CACHE_TTL_S = 600

def create_dek(db, kms, learner_id: str, kms_key_id: str) -> int:
    resp = kms.generate_data_key(KeyId=kms_key_id, KeySpec="AES_256",
                                 EncryptionContext={"learner_id": learner_id, "app": "forone"})
    db.execute("insert into learner_keys (learner_id, dek_wrapped, kms_key_id, key_version) values (%s,%s,%s,1)",
               (learner_id, resp["CiphertextBlob"], kms_key_id))
    _zero(resp["Plaintext"])
    return 1

def get_dek(db, kms, audit, learner_id: str, purpose: Purpose, actor: str) -> Dek:
    now = time.monotonic()
    hit = _cache.get(learner_id)
    if hit and hit[1] > now:
        return hit[0]
    row = db.one("select dek_wrapped, key_version, destroyed_at from learner_keys where learner_id=%s", (learner_id,))
    if row is None or row.destroyed_at is not None:
        raise KeyDestroyed(learner_id)
    plaintext = kms.decrypt(CiphertextBlob=row.dek_wrapped,
                            EncryptionContext={"learner_id": learner_id, "app": "forone"})["Plaintext"]
    dek = Dek(key=plaintext, md5_b64=base64.b64encode(hashlib.md5(plaintext).digest()).decode(),
              key_b64=base64.b64encode(plaintext).decode(), version=row.key_version)
    _cache[learner_id] = (dek, now + CACHE_TTL_S)
    audit.write(actor=actor, action="key.unwrap", learner_id=learner_id, meta={"purpose": purpose})
    return dek

def destroy(db, audit, learner_id: str, actor: str) -> None:
    _cache.pop(learner_id, None)
    db.execute("update learner_keys set destroyed_at = now() where learner_id=%s", (learner_id,))
    db.execute("delete from learner_keys where learner_id=%s", (learner_id,))
    audit.write(actor=actor, action="key.destroy", learner_id=learner_id)

def sse_c_headers(dek: Dek) -> dict:
    return {"SSECustomerAlgorithm": "AES256", "SSECustomerKey": dek.key_b64, "SSECustomerKeyMD5": dek.md5_b64}

def _zero(b: bytes) -> None:   # best effort in CPython; the point is not to keep references
    del b

The EncryptionContext binds every wrapped DEK to its learner id, so a wrapped key copied onto another learner's row cannot be unwrapped. Rule for every caller: never log Dek, never put it in an exception, never return it over an interface other than the worker's brief response (03 §4.3 crypto.dek), which is over TLS inside the VPC and authenticated by the service token.

What is not encrypted at the column level, and why. Life facts, transcripts and identity live in RDS under storage encryption and RLS only. Column-level encryption would stop the rebuild (04 D14), the quote check (04 4.7) and pgvector search for callbacks, and would need key handling in every store writer. The compensating controls are RLS, pgaudit on the human role, the audit log and the processor register. Rejected: pgcrypto on life_facts.text and events.payload (section 8).

# packages/core/forone/core/anonymize.py
import hmac, hashlib, re
from dataclasses import dataclass

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Identity:            # compiled per learner from learners.display_name and life_facts.people[*].name_used
    names: list[str]       # every spelling the learner used, Arabic and Latin, plus the hosts' respelling
    emails: list[str]

TOKENS = {"name": "{NAME}", "person": "{PERSON}", "email": "{EMAIL}", "phone": "{PHONE}", "url": "{URL}", "number": "{NUMBER}"}
RE_EMAIL = re.compile(r"[\w.+-]+@[\w-]+\.[\w.-]+")
RE_PHONE = re.compile(r"(?:\+?\d[\d\s-]{7,}\d)")
RE_URL   = re.compile(r"https?://\S+|www\.\S+")
RE_NUM5  = re.compile(r"(?<!\d)\d{5,}(?!\d)")

def pseudonym(secret: bytes, learner_id: str) -> str:
    return hmac.new(secret, learner_id.encode(), hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()

def anonymize(text: str, ident: Identity) -> str:
    out = text
    for n in sorted(ident.names, key=len, reverse=True):            # longest first so "Faisal Al-X" goes before "Faisal"
        out = re.sub(rf"(?<!\w){re.escape(n)}(?!\w)", TOKENS["name"], out, flags=re.IGNORECASE)
    out = RE_EMAIL.sub(TOKENS["email"], out)
    out = RE_URL.sub(TOKENS["url"], out)
    out = RE_PHONE.sub(TOKENS["phone"], out)
    out = RE_NUM5.sub(TOKENS["number"], out)
    return out

Rules: (1) the learner's own grammar is untouched (that is the data); (2) names of people the learner mentioned are known only when the memory keeper stored them in life_facts.people, so a person mentioned once and not stored may survive as a first name, which is why the copy says "after removing your name and the names of people you mention" and why access is a named tagger under agreement, not a dataset download; (3) place names, employers and teams stay (they are what the teacher is checked against); (4) research_transcripts.learner_ref and calibration_samples.learner_ref are the pseudonym, and learner_id never appears; (5) /admin/research renders rows for the tagger role one at a time, logs every view to audit_log with the pseudonym (not the learner id), and has no export button; (6) research/revoke deletes by pseudonym within 24 h of data_use: false, and learner/delete step 4 (04) does the same; (7) a learner's calibration_samples.audio_ref is set only when both data_use and voice_storage are true at sample time, and is nulled by learner/purge-audio (an [ASSUMPTION] on 05 that audio_ref points at a media_objects row so the purge finds it).
